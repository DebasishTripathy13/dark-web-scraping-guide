## Critical Safety Rules

### Rule 1: Always Use VPN + Tor

**Why:** In many countries, ISPs flag Tor usage as suspicious activity.

**Proper Setup:**
1. Connect to VPN **first**
2. Then connect to Tor
3. This hides your Tor usage from your ISP

**From Apurv:**
> "You definitely want to do VPN and then Tor so that your ISP can't see you are accessing Tor. In many countries, Tor is considered like, 'Okay, they must be doing something illegal.'"

### Rule 2: Never Access Illegal Marketplaces

**Do NOT search for or access:**
- Drug marketplaces
- Stolen credit card shops
- Hacking services
- Weapons dealers
- Counterfeit goods
- Any illegal marketplace

**Why:** Even accessing these sites can put you on law enforcement watchlists.

### Rule 3: Never Search for CSAM (Child Sexual Abuse Material)

**⚠️ ABSOLUTE ZERO TOLERANCE ⚠️**

**From Apurv:**
> "Especially in the US, if you search any CSAM-related stuff and you have something saved on your system, you are in big trouble. That is a full illegal area that you are going into. Even if you do it mistakenly, they will not listen to you. You are in trouble. You will get arrested. It's always better to not search for any of those things."

**Consequences:**
- Immediate arrest
- Federal charges
- Mandatory minimum sentencing
- Permanent criminal record
- No "I was just curious" defense

**Robin's Guardrails:**
- Apurv coded NSFW filters into the prompts
- These are **NOT foolproof**
- You must still be extremely careful

### Rule 4: Never Download Anything from Dark Web Sites

**Rule of Thumb:** Don't download anything. Period.

**Risks:**
- Malware
- Ransomware
- Stealer trojans
- Keyloggers
- Remote access tools (RATs)

Even "legitimate-looking" files can be weaponized.

### Rule 5: No Suspicious Purchases

**Do NOT:**
- Buy anything from dark web sites
- Share your cryptocurrency addresses
- Provide payment information
- "Test" marketplaces with small purchases

Law enforcement monitors transactions. Even test purchases can be prosecuted.

## Privacy Protection

### VPN Selection

**Choose a VPN that:**
- Has a strict no-logs policy
- Is located in a privacy-friendly jurisdiction
- Accepts anonymous payment methods
- Has been audited by third parties

**Recommended Features:**
- Kill switch (disconnects internet if VPN drops)
- DNS leak protection
- Multi-hop connections (optional, for extra paranoia)

### Browser Safety

**Best Practices:**
1. Use Tor Browser or a dedicated browser
2. Never use your regular browser for dark web access
3. Disable JavaScript in Tor Browser (Settings → Security → Safest)
4. Clear cookies and cache regularly
5. Never log into personal accounts while on Tor

### Device Isolation

**Ideal Setup:**
- Dedicated machine for dark web research
- Separate from your personal/work computers
- Run Linux (more secure than Windows)
- Use full disk encryption

**Minimum Setup:**
- Separate user account on your machine
- Never mix personal and research activities
- Use Docker containers for additional isolation

## Professional Investigator Guidelines

### Sock Puppet Accounts

If you're conducting professional threat research, you'll need fake accounts.

**Requirements:**
- Create accounts NOT tied to your real identity
- Use burner phone numbers (no real phone)
- Use separate email addresses
- **CRITICAL:** Never use your real email as a recovery email

**From Apurv:**
> "A lot of beginners, when they try to create these accounts, they put recovery email as their main email. [Don't] even think about putting something related to you."

### Persona Management

**Creating a Believable Persona:**

1. **Make it real and consistent**
   - Choose a name, location, background
   - Remember all details
   - Write everything down

2. **Adopt a consistent communication style**
   - Speaking patterns
   - Language/slang usage
   - Topics of interest
   - Timezone/activity hours

3. **Never break character**
   - Stay in persona at all times
   - Don't slip up with personal details
   - Threat actors are watching for inconsistencies

4. **Keep detailed notes**
   - Track what you've said
   - Document interactions
   - Maintain timeline of activities

5. **Don't mix personas**
   - One persona per account/identity
   - Never combine multiple personas
   - Use separate browsers/containers for each

**From Apurv:**
> "If you are actually talking to threat actors, you have to be very wary of what you say. New name, new me, new everything, the way you talk, the accent you have. And you want to make sure you remember those details you create, because these dark web criminals, they're not dumb. They're anticipating cops getting in there or researchers coming in, and they're looking for signs of inconsistencies."

### Threat Actor Detection

**They're Looking For:**
- Inconsistencies in your story
- Timezone mismatches
- Language/cultural mistakes
- Too many questions
- Overeagerness to access private areas

**Consequences of Being Detected:**
- Doxxing (personal information exposed)
- Swatting (false police reports to your address)
- Targeted harassment
- Physical threats

**From Apurv:**
> "The thing is, threat actors can sometimes swat you, they can dox you. Once they dox you, you are out in the open. These people can then contact other people in your network and make your life hell."

### Investigation Timeline

**Reality Check:**
- Real investigations take **days, weeks, or months**
- You must build trust over time
- Sites may only be operational 2 days per week
- You might need to move through multiple forums before reaching private Telegram groups
- Patience is absolutely essential

**From Apurv:**
> "You have to stay there, build trust. You have to kind of go undercover like an undercover cop."

## If You Make a Mistake

### Accidentally Accessed Illegal Content

1. **Do NOT screenshot or save**
2. **Close the browser immediately**
3. **Clear your browser cache and cookies**
4. **Do NOT tell anyone except a lawyer**
5. **Consider consulting a lawyer**
6. **Document the accident (time, how it happened)**

### Received Threats or Doxxed

1. **Document everything** (screenshots, messages, timestamps)
2. **Do NOT engage with the threat actor**
3. **Report to local law enforcement**
4. **Contact FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)** (US)
5. **Consider changing addresses/phone numbers**
6. **Enable two-factor authentication on all accounts**
